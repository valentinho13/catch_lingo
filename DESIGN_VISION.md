# CatchLingo Design Vision

> **Canonical design authority.** This is the single source of truth for how
> CatchLingo looks and feels. Where any other document (DESIGN_SYSTEM.md, UI_SPEC.md,
> theme code) disagrees on color, atmosphere, or emotional direction, **this file wins**
> and the other should be corrected to match.
>
> The previous cool/indigo-and-mint, dark-camera direction is **retired**. CatchLingo is
> warm, sunlit, green-and-amber on a cream canvas. Do not reintroduce cool, dark, or
> technical-HUD aesthetics.

> The visual and emotional manifesto for CatchLingo.
> Read this before designing any screen, animation, illustration, or piece of copy.
> It describes how CatchLingo should *feel* — not how to build it.

CatchLingo is not a course, a deck of cards, or a productivity tool with a streak.
It is a **field journal for language found in the real world**. The person points
their attention at the life around them — a coffee on a windowsill, a chair, a
street — and walks away holding a small, beautiful piece of that moment, now named
in a new language. The product exists to make *noticing the world* feel valuable.

Everything below protects that feeling.

---

## 1. The Core Idea: Two Worlds, One Gesture

CatchLingo lives at the meeting point of two visual worlds, and its entire identity
comes from the **contrast between them**:

- **The Real World** — warm, photographic, sunlit, lived-in. Soft depth of field,
  golden light, real surfaces: wood, paper, plants, ceramic, morning haze. This is
  where words are found. It is never sterile, never cold, never a technical camera feed.

- **The Found Object** — a single, crisp, calm card that holds the word. Clean,
  quiet, weighted, slightly elevated above the scene. It is the moment of clarity
  lifted out of the noise of reality.

The signature CatchLingo gesture is **placing a clean specimen card over a warm
photograph of the world** — like pinning a pressed flower, laying a museum label
beside an object, or holding up a Polaroid of a memory. That single contrast is the
brand. If a screen ever loses one of these two worlds, it stops being CatchLingo.

---

## 2. Emotional Identity

The design should consistently produce a small, specific set of feelings:

**Warmth.** The person should feel welcomed, the way a sunlit room or a good morning
feels welcoming. Warmth is the emotional baseline, not a decoration.

**Quiet wonder.** Discovering a word should feel like noticing something you walked
past a hundred times and finally *saw*. Gentle delight, never hype.

**Ownership.** A caught word is *yours*. It belongs to you, it came from your day,
from your world. The interface should make the person feel they are gathering a
personal, meaningful collection — not completing assignments.

**Calm confidence.** The product is unhurried and sure of itself. It never nags,
never shames, never shouts. It trusts the person to return because the experience
felt good, not because a counter is about to break.

What we are explicitly *not* trying to evoke: pressure, urgency, competition,
obligation, test anxiety, or childish over-stimulation.

---

## 3. Visual Identity — What Makes It Recognizable

CatchLingo should be recognizable from a single glance, even cropped or blurred,
because of a handful of unmistakable signatures:

- The **warm real-world backdrop** with natural, directional light.
- The **single clean card** carrying one word, floating with soft, real shadow.
- A **living green** of discovery paired with a **warm amber** of reward, set on a
  **cream** canvas.
- A **friendly, curious companion** — soft, hand-drawn, more pet than teacher.
- **Hand-crafted little illustrations** for each found word, with the tactile charm
  of a sticker album or a naturalist's drawings.
- A restful sense of **space**: one hero per screen, generous breathing room.

These are not styling choices layered on top of the product. They *are* the product's
face.

---

## 4. Product Personality

If CatchLingo were a physical object, it would be a **beautifully made pocket field
journal** — the kind a thoughtful traveler carries. Linen-bound, warm paper, a few
pressed specimens and hand-drawn notes inside, a ribbon to mark your place. Personal,
tactile, a little romantic, quietly premium.

If CatchLingo were a place, it would be a **sunlit café table mid-morning**: calm,
unrushed, full of small things worth looking at, with good light coming through the
window.

If CatchLingo were a person, it would be a **warm, curious companion** who walks
beside you and gently points things out — never a teacher at the front of a class,
never a coach with a stopwatch, never a slot machine.

The brand is editorial and lifestyle in spirit — closer to a well-designed lifestyle
magazine than to an ed-tech app or a gaming UI.

---

## 5. Color Philosophy

Color carries emotional roles, not visual variety. Each hue means something, and
restraint is the rule.

**Cream and warm neutrals are the canvas.** They make the product feel soft, calm,
and premium. The default state of any screen is warm and quiet — not stark white and
not dark.

**Green is life and discovery.** It marks the act of finding, the alive thing, the
"go." It is fresh and growing, never neon, never a clinical "system success" green.
Green says: *something here is worth noticing.*

**Amber is reward and treasure.** It marks the moment of value — the caught word, the
small token of progress, the warmth of the companion. Amber is the glow of having
found something good. It is used sparingly, so it always feels precious.

The palette should always feel like it belongs to a warm, naturally lit world.
Cool, corporate blues and high-saturation primaries do not belong here; they would
make the product feel like software instead of a souvenir.

---

## 6. Lighting Philosophy

Warmth in CatchLingo is created primarily by **light**, and this is deliberate.

The world behind the words is always lit like **golden hour or a soft sunlit
morning** — directional, warm, with gentle shadows and a little atmosphere. This
light is the single biggest reason the product feels emotional rather than
functional. It signals *a real, lived moment*, not a captured data frame.

Even the clean specimen cards sit in that light: soft, believable shadows give them
weight and presence, as if they are real objects resting on a real surface. Light is
how CatchLingo says "this is a memory," not "this is a screen."

Flat, shadowless, evenly lit interfaces would drain the product of its soul. Harsh,
cold, or clinical lighting would make it feel like a tool.

---

## 7. Information Density

CatchLingo is **deliberately quiet**. The guiding rule is *one hero per screen*.

When catching, there is one word. When celebrating, there is one revealed word. When
reviewing, there is one card. Supporting information — counts, context, progress —
exists but stays small, soft, and secondary. It never crowds the moment.

This restraint is not minimalism for its own sake; it is how the product creates
*focus and reverence*. A single word given room to breathe feels valuable. The same
word in a dense list feels like inventory. Generous negative space is one of
CatchLingo's strongest premium and emotional signals — protect it fiercely.

Dashboards, dense grids, multi-column data, and walls of numbers are the opposite of
this product.

---

## 8. Premium Signals — Why It Feels Valuable, Not Cheap

The product earns the feeling of value through quiet craft:

- **Space and restraint.** Few elements, confidently placed, with room around them.
- **Real depth and soft shadow.** Objects feel physical and present, not flat.
- **Honest, beautiful light.** Warm, directional, atmospheric — never stock-photo cold.
- **A disciplined palette** where every color means something.
- **Tactile, hand-made detail** in illustrations and the companion, signaling care.
- **Calm, editorial typography** — clear, confident, unfussy.
- **Honesty as a feature.** Even acknowledging that a capability is an early prototype
  is done plainly and gracefully; transparency reads as self-assurance, not weakness.

Cheapness would come from clutter, garish color, fake juice, harsh contrast, and
generic flat icons. CatchLingo avoids all of these.

---

## 9. Discovery Signals — Communicating Exploration

The act of *finding* must be felt in the visual language:

- The **real world is present and full-bleed** — the person is looking *through* the
  app at their surroundings, not at an abstract menu.
- A **light framing gesture** suggests focus and attention being directed at the
  world, without ever becoming a technical heads-up display.
- **Morning and sunlight motifs** — a sense of a day beginning, the world ready to be
  explored.
- Language that invites looking outward: *find, notice, spot, explore your world* —
  never *study, drill, complete*.

Discovery should feel like gently lifting your eyes to what's around you, not like
scanning a database.

---

## 10. Collection Signals — Communicating Ownership

Collecting is the motivator, and it must feel like **keeping treasured findings** — not
accumulating points:

- The **caught word becomes a clean, held object** — a specimen, a souvenir, a card
  worth keeping.
- A **small warm token** marks the moment a word is added — a quiet, satisfying "you
  now have this," not a loud score.
- The **collection is a personal field journal**: each entry remembers not just the
  word, but *where and when* it was found. Context is what turns a word into a memory.
- **Gentle sets and categories** give the collection shape and a soft sense of
  completeness, framed as gathering, never as quotas to grind.
- Words are shown as **warm, individual illustrated specimens**, giving each one
  character and collectible charm.

Collecting in CatchLingo is the joy of a souvenir shelf, not the pressure of a
progress bar.

---

## 11. Celebration Philosophy

The moment a word is caught is the emotional peak of the product, and it is handled
with **gentle magic, not spectacle**.

The translation is *revealed*, not merely displayed — it arrives as a small reward for
the act of catching. A soft sparkle of light, a warm token, a quiet sense of "yes."
The celebration is tactile and alive, but it stays graceful and brief. It feels like
delight, never like a casino.

Emotion always comes before mechanics. The person should remember *how good it felt to
catch that word*, not how many points it was worth.

---

## Elements That MUST Stay — The CatchLingo Identity

These have become load-bearing parts of who CatchLingo is. Removing any one of them
weakens the brand:

- The **warm, sunlit, real-world backdrop** with natural light and depth.
- The **single clean specimen card** lifted over that world — the signature contrast.
- The **green-of-discovery / amber-of-reward / cream-canvas** emotional palette.
- The **friendly hand-drawn companion** as a warm guide, not a teacher.
- **Soft, tactile, hand-crafted illustrations** for individual words and categories.
- A **light, attention-framing discovery gesture** over the real world.
- **Low information density** — one hero per screen, with room to breathe.
- **Where-and-when context** attached to every collected word.
- The **reveal-as-reward** moment of catching.
- A **calm, honest, confident voice** in tone and copy.

## Elements That Would Destroy This Direction — Avoid

Any of these would break CatchLingo's identity and should be treated as red flags:

- **Cold, technical AR / HUD aesthetics**: dark sci-fi overlays, bounding boxes,
  confidence percentages, debug readouts, neon-on-black.
- **School or flashcard energy**: rigid tables, dense word lists, red/green
  right-wrong pressure, grades, scores, test framing.
- **Childish gamification**: loud confetti, oversized cartoon mascots, dominant
  coin/XP bars, badges everywhere, garish saturated color.
- **Productivity-tool density**: dashboards, charts, many small numbers, streak-shaming.
- **Cold or clinical palettes**: corporate blues, sterile pure-white screens, harsh
  shadows, flat shadowless design.
- **Cheap juice**: over-animation, fake gloss, heavy skeuomorphism, effects that draw
  attention to themselves instead of the word.
- **Stock-photo coldness**: flat, evenly lit, soulless imagery. The warmth and the
  light are not optional.

---

## Reconciliation — How This Vision Was Settled

CatchLingo had an earlier visual direction encoded in its foundations: a cooler
indigo-led identity with a mint accent, and an Explore experience that leaned on a
dark, technical camera surface with status overlays and confidence-style readouts.
That direction was competent but read as *software* — a capable tool rather than a
warm, valuable find. The strongest direction reconciles that history into a single,
coherent vision.

**What changed.** The emotional center of gravity moved from *cool and technical* to
*warm and lived-in*. Discovery is now expressed through warm real-world light and a
gentle framing gesture, rather than through a dark, instrument-like camera view.
Reward and ownership gained a distinct **warm amber** voice alongside the green of
discovery, so that *finding* and *keeping* feel emotionally different. The collection
was reframed from a stored word list into a **personal field journal** with place and
time. Celebration shifted from a status confirmation toward a small, magical reveal.

**What stayed.** The product loop — Discover, Collect, Celebrate, Review — remains the
spine. The idea of two layers, the real-world layer and the language layer, stays and
is now expressed as the warm-world / clean-card contrast. The commitment to *honesty*
about an early prototype stays, but is now framed as quiet confidence rather than a
technical caveat. A green discovery accent and a calm, uncluttered interface remain.

**What was removed.** The cold, technical reading of the camera as an instrument
panel; any reliance on confidence numbers, debug-style overlays, or HUD language as
visual identity; and the assumption that a cooler, more corporate palette could carry
the brand. These are now explicitly off-limits because they pull the product back
toward feeling like a tool.

**Which principles became stronger.** Warmth-through-light is now a first-class
principle, not an afterthought. Restraint and low information density are elevated from
"clean UI" to a deliberate source of reverence and premium feel. The collection's
emotional meaning — context, ownership, souvenir — is stated more strongly than the
mechanics of counting. And the guiding test for any future decision is sharper than
before:

> **Does this make noticing the world feel more valuable — or does it make CatchLingo
> feel more like an app?**

When in doubt, choose warmth, choose space, choose the feeling of a treasured find.
